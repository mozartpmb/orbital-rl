The dynamics bug — found twice independently

true_to_mean() in the C env had its half-angle factors swapped (forward E→θ map applied instead of its inverse). It's reached only after a burn, so every burn teleported the spacecraft along-track by ≈ 2e·sin θ — measured ~707 km one step after a 10 m/s burn at θ=90°, e=0.05, and a median 24.5° of free phase shift per episode on logged trajectories. Coast dynamics were always exact; apsidal burns are unaffected to first order, which is why five months of Hohmann-style policies never exposed it. The EKF port audit caught it by inspection; the Lambert baseline caught it independently because the policy was beating the two-impulse optimum — an impossibility. Fixed and verified (0.4 m residual); commit f55d9cb.

Consequences, measured: the canonical checkpoint drops 97.5% → 13.0% under corrected dynamics. Fresh Stage-1 bootstrap fails at both 40M and 80M steps (the bug had been subsidizing exploration; the Φ_orbit shaping term actively penalizes the drift-orbit strategy that correct physics requires for phasing). Best recovery so far: gentle re-adaptation of the legacy policy → 33.5% greedy (interim checkpoint committed to the registry). Low-lr crystallization and a curriculum rebuild both failed. Full detail: T1_DYNAMICS_FIX_FINDINGS.md.

01 · Verified spec

What you actually built

Everything here was read directly from the working-tree source or measured against the compiled environment (file:line citations available in the full audit reports).

Environment (C, PufferLib Ocean pattern)

  • Dynamics: 2D coplanar two-body, analytic Kepler propagation with Newton–Raphson on Kepler's equation. Verified numerically clean at all altitudes: worst drift 2×10⁻⁷ m over 100 GEO periods. No J2, drag, SRP, or attitude.
  • Maneuvering: impulsive burns with Tsiolkovsky depletion. Isp 300 s, 850 kg dry / 150 kg fuel → 478 m/s Δv budget.
  • Task: rendezvous with an independently propagated moving target from randomized initial orbits. Success = simultaneous dist < 30 km AND |v_rel| < 50 m/s (orbital.h:788). Reward on success: +5..+10 scaled by remaining fuel; all failures −10. The |Δa| < 10 km, e < 0.01 criterion in CLAUDE.md and RECIPE.md's version are both wrong — those constants survive only inside the shaping potential.
  • Terminations: success / collision / escape / stranded / 2000-step cap / gave-up-init (reward 0). The escape branch is dead code: escaping from 500 km needs 3,155 m/s against a 478 m/s budget (break-even radius ≈ 299,000 km). Zero escapes in 6,000 logged episodes.
  • Observations: 38 dims — chaser elements (7), target (2), ω sin/cos (4), phase terms (4), nearest-body block (16), LVLH relative state (5). Under the standing no-debris recipe the 16-dim body block carries zero information: 12 dims identically 0, one constant, and three that are exact functions of other dims (one is a byte-identical duplicate). Verified numerically over 500 steps.
  • Actions: env now exposes Discrete(16) — coast; prograde/retrograde ±5/10/25 m/s; radial ±10 m/s; warp 5 min/30 min/1 hr; fine ±1/±2 m/s. The deliverable checkpoints have 10-logit heads — they were trained when only Discrete(10) existed. Actions 10–15 aren't masked; they didn't exist. The 30-min/1-hr warps and fine burns have never been exercised by any training or eval run.
  • Reward shaping: gated potential-based shaping (orbit / phase / velocity components) with a terminal Φ-clamp; correctly ordered after termination checks. Discounting is inconsistent under time-warp: the shaping applies γτ for a τ-substep warp, but GAE applies one flat γ=0.995 per transition. Result: 96.8% of all actions the converged policy takes are warp-5min; coast goes unused.
  • Trainer: PPO + LSTM, Muon optimizer, γ=0.995, 1,024 envs, Multiprocessing vec backend (the root cause of the fixed-seed nondeterminism finding).

Everything else labeled "eccentricity" in the repo is confounded: the e_max scan axis collapses to the same e ≤ 0.084 distribution from 0.10 upward (LEO geometry caps eccentricity — perigee must clear the atmosphere); the published degradation at "e = 0.5/0.7" was doomed-init contamination from a since-fixed 256-attempt sampler cap (pass-only success at "e = 0.70" was 97.1%); and the infamous "cliff at e ≥ 0.075" from the wrap-up was mostly an altitude confound — its eval script rescaled the altitude band by 1/(1−e), silently testing far outside the training band. That single fact explains most of the Phase 5 confusion.

Why "prepare for higher altitude / higher e" kept failing — four verified blockers

  1. Period budget vs the 2000-step cap (physics). At GEO the agent gets 1.4 orbits to solve a multi-revolution phasing problem. The fix (M4: raise MAX_STEPS) was specced and never landed.
  2. Reward leak at wide altitude bands (metrology, and a live training hazard). On a timeout the terminal Φ-clamp adds a positive term proportional to how far the sat is from target; with the eval's reward > 0 success classifier, a timeout scores as "success" whenever |Δa| > 10,000 km. The audit reproduced the reported GEO "capability" with a policy that never acts (53/100 coast-only "successes" at GEO). Worse: for any training band wider than ~17,000 km, freeze-and-time-out earns ~+300 return versus ~+10 for succeeding — the reward's global optimum is doing nothing. Unfixed.
  3. Plasticity lock (RL). Converged checkpoints cannot be fine-tuned: aggressive hyperparameters destroy the policy globally (LEO → 0%), gentle ones preserve it but learn nothing, mid-training warm-starts trade LEO regression for tiny lift. Twelve runs across four protocols; fifth independent reproduction of this pattern in the project. Caveat: the sweep was narrow — re-bootstrapping per band Stage-1.0-style, KL-constrained updates, and freezing were never tried.
  4. Geometric infeasibility at LEO high-e (physics). e ≥ 0.084 has no valid initial conditions in the trained band. The "wall" Phase 5c/5d spent ~50 compute-hours attacking was 64–94% physically impossible tasks.
The unfalsified conclusion

The docs' final verdict — "the recipe is structurally bounded; ship the LEO agent and close" — was reached without ever using the tools built to test it: every Phase 5.5 run pinned legacy_action_space=10, so the longer warps (M2) and fine burns (M3) engineered specifically for MEO/GEO were exercised by zero runs, and MAX_STEPS was never raised (M4). "Structurally bounded" is unfalsified, not confirmed. This is simultaneously an honest interview talking point and the highest-value open research thread.

04 · Narrative

The story arc — ten beats to have fluent

MAR–APR 2026

Build. C env + PufferLib binding; physics validated against textbook Hohmann values; ~500–700k steps/sec. Circular transfers solved to ~100% with clean two-impulse apsidal burns. Debris deliberately shelved — it masked the transfer structure you were trying to learn.

You pivoted the task to rendezvous — position + velocity against a propagated target — which is both harder and better-posed. Honest note: the wall was routed around, not solved.

APR 25

Phase 4: curriculum cracks 180° phasing. Of four interventions, only the training schedule (30°→90°→180° curriculum + a time-warp action) worked: 79.6% multi-seed. Ablations showed LVLH observations and the shaping were load-bearing for the curriculum even though they looked inert standalone.

APR 28

The determinism finding. Identical seed, config, and code gave 5% / 40% / 77% — the Multiprocessing vec backend makes minibatch order OS-scheduling-dependent. Consequence: multi-seed minimum for all structural claims. This is a systems-level debugging story interviewers love.

APR 28–30

Phase 5b: the working recipe. Random satellite-eccentricity init + a two-stage curriculum (bootstrap on same-orbit phasing, then randomize). The three-arm test — fresh 0%, warm-from-specialist 1.2%, warm-from-broad 61.2% — proved curriculum is required, not helpful. Emergent behavior: the agent exploits non-uniform angular velocity on eccentric orbits to phase faster.

APR 30–MAY 1

50 compute-hours debugging a phantom. MAY 2

Verification catches two more confounds. You noticed web-frontend trajectories starting inside the Earth and pushed instead of dismissing it. MAY 21

Phase 5.5 in one day. Env mods for altitude (LVLH scaling, longer warps, fine burns) landed; the only training attempt collapsed — converged checkpoints have no usable plasticity (four fine-tune protocols, no window without LEO regression). Zero-shot probes suggested ~50% envelope capability.

MAY 21–22

The Φ-clamp leak — the ninth and biggest metric bug. Real MEO/GEO capability ≈ 0. The honest deliverable: a LEO/MEO-low phasing specialist whose skill decays with orbital period.

MAY 22 → AUG

Stopped mid-correction. The six recommended closure steps (patch classifier, fix clamp, re-audit, update docs, commit) were never executed. That's the state you're returning to — and Tier 0 of the plan below is exactly those steps.

06 · Presentation

How to frame it for Draper

The headline, stated honestly

"I built a two-body orbital rendezvous environment in C — analytic Kepler propagation, Tsiolkovsky fuel, ~500k steps/sec across 1,024 instances — and trained a recurrent PPO policy that solves randomized coplanar LEO rendezvous at 94% mean success across five training seeds (88–97.5%), against a deliberately coarse far-field criterion of 30 km and 50 m/s. The only failure mode is timing out: zero collisions, escapes, or fuel-outs in a thousand evaluated episodes. The policy costs about 2.5× Hohmann Δv — it buys robustness with propellant — and the project's hardest lessons weren't in the RL, they were in verification: I documented nine separate cases where a metric measured something different from its name, and the discipline that caught them is the part I'm proudest of."

That last clause is the differentiator. Draper's interview style is project interrogation, and the req's duties are half verification and documentation. You have a genuinely unusual asset: a paper trail of retractions you caught yourself — the impossible-trajectories catch (you noticed satellites starting inside Earth and refused to write it off as a rendering bug), the doomed-init discovery that turned a "capability wall" into a one-line env fix, the Simpson's-paradox catch, the Φ-clamp leak. Lead with limitations, as the review says — but frame them as findings from your own verification process, not confessions.

Structure the talk as the req describes the job

Problem → requirements → design trades → implementation → verification → what I'd do differently. Your project maps beat-for-beat: the trade studies are real (discrete vs continuous actions, timestep resolution, curriculum design, analytic vs numerical propagation — each with data on why the alternative failed), and "what I'd do differently" writes itself (tighten the criterion, γτ discounting, validate the env before debugging the reward, metadata that records outcomes not intent).

3 · "Why analytic Kepler instead of an integrator?"
Exactness at any dt (which makes time-warp actions free), no energy drift over 2,000-step episodes, and speed. Trade: it hard-codes two-body, so J2/drag/n-body require switching to RK4 — a deliberate scope decision, and the planned cislunar extension is where the integrator becomes necessary.
